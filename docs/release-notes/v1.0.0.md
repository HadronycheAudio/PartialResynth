The first release of PartialResynth, a VST® 3 effect that listens to its input, finds the five loudest partials, and plays each of them with its own synth voice. The analysis is a port of `fiddle~` from Pure Data, by Miller Puckette and Ted Apel.

## What's in it

- **Analysis:** each frame's peaks match the original `fiddle~`, with a 2048-sample window. A new frame arrives every 512 samples, twice as often as in the original, for less latency.
- **Five voices that follow their partials:** a voice stays with its partial as it moves and changes level, and there are no envelopes to set: the analysis is the envelope. All the controls apply to all five voices.
- **Two oscillators per voice:** sine, saw or square, each with its own level and tuning relative to the partial.
- **A filter per voice:** low-pass, high-pass, band-pass or notch, with resonance, key follow, and an amount that lets each partial's level move its own cutoff.
- **Glide:** slows the pitch down as the voices follow their partials.
- **Spread:** places the voices across the stereo field.
- **Dry/wet:** the dry signal is delayed to line up with the voices.

The latency is 1536 samples at 44.1 and 48 kHz (35 and 32 ms), and it's reported to the host.

## Installing

Download `PartialResynth-1.0.0-Setup.exe` below and run it. It installs to `C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3`, which is where DAWs look by default, and you can remove it from Windows' Installed apps.

**Windows will probably warn you when you run it,** because the installer isn't code-signed. To continue, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

## Requirements

- Windows 10 or later, 64-bit
- A VST 3 host. PartialResynth is developed for Cubase, and other hosts haven't been tried.
